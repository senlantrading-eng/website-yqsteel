China Steel Exports Stay Above 10 Million Tonnes, Raising the Value of Product Choice

China’s finished-steel exports remained above 10 million tonnes in September, extending a run of heavy outward flow. For overseas buyers, this creates a wider procurement menu—but the best outcome still depends on matching the right product brief to a port-ready shipment plan.

1) A sustained export flow broadens the finished-steel menu.

Shanghai Metals Market reported that China exported 10.492 million tonnes of steel in September, 3.3% above August, while Bloomberg reported a fifth consecutive month above the 10-million-tonne mark. That sustained pace keeps supply choices broad across coil, plate, sections, bar and coated products.

Availability, however, is only the starting point. Buyers should use the wider choice to compare material against the actual job requirement: grade, standard, dimensions, tolerance, coating, length and pack format. A general market signal cannot replace a precise purchase specification.

2) Product mix should be tested before a quote becomes an order.

Each product family carries its own acceptance and handling variables. Flat products should be checked for width, thickness tolerance, surface condition and mill test certificate; long products need equally clear confirmation of standard, length, bundle arrangement and identification.

A concise order sheet makes those checks practical. It should state the finished product, applicable standard, grade, size range, quantity, destination port, requested shipment window, packing requirement and any destination-specific documentation. This lets a supplier align mill allocation and logistics around an executable requirement.

3) High volumes make port discipline more—not less—important.

Strong export flow can provide options, but it also raises the importance of booking and cut-off discipline. Export orders that look competitive at the mill can lose time if packing, marking, inspection instructions, document drafts and vessel timing are left until cargo is at the port.

For coil and coated products, moisture protection, edge protection and secure lashing should be agreed before loading. Buyers should also confirm the document set early, including invoice, packing list, mill certificate and any destination certificates. The objective is to preserve the commercial value of the chosen product through the final export step.

4) YQ Steel’s working view for October 24.

China’s continued high export flow gives procurement teams room to choose a more suitable finished-steel mix. The stronger buying position comes from treating the specification, quality evidence and shipping plan as one decision—not as separate steps after price agreement.
