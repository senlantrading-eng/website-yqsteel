China Steel Exports Hold Above 10 Million Tonnes as Buyers Focus on Product Mix

China's September finished-steel exports strengthened again, keeping supply options broad for overseas buyers. The useful question is no longer simply whether material is available, but which product and shipping plan can be executed cleanly.

1) High export flow is widening the buying menu.

Shanghai Metals Market reported that China exported 10.492 million tonnes of steel in September, up 3.3% from August, supported by improved export orders and a softer yuan. Separately, Bloomberg reported that Chinese steel exports had exceeded 10 million tonnes for a fifth consecutive month.

That scale keeps a wide range of finished products in circulation: hot-rolled coil, plate, rebar, wire rod, sections and coated sheet. It also means buyers can make a more deliberate comparison between grade, tolerance, coating and pack format instead of treating a general availability signal as a substitute for a specification.

2) Product selection should lead the price conversation.

Export competitiveness is not uniform across products. A coil quote should be assessed alongside width, thickness tolerance, surface condition, mill test certificate and corrosion protection; long products need the corresponding attention to standard, bundle configuration and cut length. Those details shape both destination acceptance and handling cost.

For buyers comparing offers, a workable order sheet should identify the finished product, standard, grade, dimensions, quantity, destination port and requested shipment window. This gives the supplier a concrete basis to align mill allocation and logistics rather than simply quote a broad market indication.

3) Execution remains part of the commercial decision.

High outbound volumes can support choice, but they also make booking discipline more valuable. The SMM outlook points to continued support for exports in October, while recent market coverage has emphasized the resilience of Chinese shipments despite trade-policy pushback in overseas markets.

Before confirming cargo, buyers should settle packing, marking, inspection, document requirements and vessel cut-off timing. For coil and coated products especially, moisture protection and secure lashing should be agreed before the cargo reaches the port. A low FOB figure has limited value if the documentation or loading plan is still unresolved.

4) YQ Steel's working view for October 22.

China's high export volume gives procurement teams a genuine opportunity to choose the right finished-steel mix. The best orders will pair that opportunity with a disciplined product brief and an executable shipping plan.
